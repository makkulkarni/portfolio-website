PMFBY Actuarial & Simulation Methodology

Formal framework explaining the mathematical derivations, Monte Carlo yield shock simulation, and portfolio aggregation mechanics.

1. Yield Shortfall Payout Equation

For each Insurance Unit (IU) $i$ and simulated season $s$, the yield payout ratio is computed against the contractual Threshold Yield $TY_i$ and indemnity factor $(1 - \text{indemnity})$:
$$\text{Payout Ratio}_{i,s} = \min\left(1, \max\left(0, \frac{TY_i \times (1 - \text{indemnity}) - Y_{i,s}}{TY_i} \right) \r\right)$$ Currency liabilities are bounded by the contractual liability cap:
$$\text{Payout}_{i,s} = \min\left(\text{Payout Ratio}_{i,s} \times \text{SI}_i \times \text{Area}_i, \text{Cap}_i \times \text{SI}_i \times \text{Area}_i \r\right)$$

2. Normal Shock Monte Carlo Sampling

Historical yields per Insurance Unit provide empirical $\mu_i$ (mean yield) and $\sigma_i$ (standard deviation). Zero-variance records receive an actuarial minimum variance buffer of $10\% \times \mu_i$. Yields are simulated via independent normal random shocks:
$$Y_{i,s} = \max(0, \mu_i + Z_s \cdot \sigma_i), \quad Z_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$$

3. Hierarchical Aggregation & Portfolio Effect

Rather than averaging individual burn rates, total payout currency is summed simultaneously across all IUs for each simulation scenario $s$:
$$\text{Burn Rate}_{C,s} = \frac{\sum_{i \in C} \text{Payout}_{i,s}}{\sum_{i \in C} \text{Total SI}_i}$$ Because high yields in some IUs offset shortfalls in others, the portfolio standard deviation $\sigma_C$ is strictly lower than the sum of individual components—demonstrating the Portfolio Pooling Effect.

4. Value at Risk (VaR) & Tail Metrics

Percentile payouts ($P_{90}$ and $P_{95}$) represent the 1-in-10 and 1-in-20 year worst-case losses. Coefficient of Variation ($CV = \frac{\sigma}{\mu}$) quantifies relative pricing risk.
